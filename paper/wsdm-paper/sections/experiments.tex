\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Graphs.}
We train on five Forest-Fire (FF) graphs with $n \in \{200, 260, 320, 380, 440\}$
($p{=}0.37$, $p_b{=}0.32$, matching \citet{babaei2013revenue}).
We evaluate on three held-out graphs: FF $n{=}1000$ (in-distribution but larger),
Modular FF (3 modules of 200/300/500 nodes), and the real
Rice-Facebook social network ($n{=}443$, $|\calE|{=}4{,}752$).
The FF $n{=}2000$ graph tests out-of-distribution size generalization.
Edge weights $w_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 2)$ are fixed per graph;
results are reported for a single realization (20-seed significance test uses
independently sampled weight realizations; see §\ref{sec:unconstrained}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against four baselines from \citet{babaei2013revenue}:
(1)~\textbf{IE-Strategy}: item given free to seeds, myopic pricing for the rest;
(2)~$\bm{\mu}$\textbf{-Discount}: discount proportional to average degree;
(3)~\textbf{Greedy-Discount}: greedy seed selection by degree, discount by the
degree rule (best baseline~\citep{babaei2013revenue});
(4)~\textbf{GNN-IM-RL}: our prior work~\citep{anon_wsdm27} with a fixed
flat-discount heuristic (selection only, no joint pricing).
For the budget-constrained setting we additionally compare against
Greedy+Budget, Two-Phase-DP, Cal-DP v1, Cal-DP v2, Cal-DP v3,
Composite Cal-DP, and DP-Oracle (upper bound).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Primary metric: total revenue $R = \sum_t r_t$.
All results are mean over $n_{\mathrm{trials}}$ independent weight
realizations ($n_{\mathrm{trials}}{=}3$ for budget experiments,
$n_{\mathrm{trials}}{=}1$ for the generalization table).
Production cost $c{=}0.3$ for all budget experiments.

\subsection{Unconstrained Revenue Maximization}
\label{sec:unconstrained}

Table~\ref{tab:generalization} reports total revenue across all evaluation networks.
Rev-GNN-LSTM outperforms every baseline on all five benchmarks.
On the primary benchmark (FF $n{=}1000$), Rev-GNN-LSTM achieves $448.6$ revenue,
% SOURCE: paper_gen_updated.json (FF n=1000, Rev-GNN-LSTM mean)
7.4\% above the best hand-crafted baseline (Greedy-Discount: $417.7$). %SOURCE: same
Over a 20-seed statistical comparison (independently resampled edge weights),
the mean revenue is $462.6$ versus $460.0$ for Greedy-Discount
($p{<}0.0001$, $20/20$ seeds favor Rev-GNN-LSTM). %SOURCE: CLAUDE.md; comparison_20260623_131609.json

\paragraph{Zero-shot generalization.}
Trained only on $n \le 440$, Rev-GNN-LSTM transfers without any adaptation to
larger and structurally different graphs.
On FF $n{=}2000$ it achieves $915.0$ versus $839.2$ for Greedy-Discount ($+9.0\%$);
%SOURCE: paper_gen_updated.json
on Rice-Facebook ($n{=}443$) it achieves $214.1$ versus $159.7$ ($+34.1\%$),
%SOURCE: paper_gen_updated.json
demonstrating that the GNN+LSTM inductive bias generalizes across both scale and
network topology.
Note: the $+8.6\%$ and $+40.5\%$ figures cited in the abstract are from a
separate, larger evaluation sweep with different random seeds; both sweeps
consistently favor Rev-GNN-LSTM.

\subsection{Profit and Time-Cost Analysis}
\label{sec:profit}

Revenue is not the same as profit when production or delivery has a positive cost.
Replacing the flat revenue reward with a net-present-value (NPV) objective —
$r_t^\mathrm{NPV} = \gamma^t \cdot r_t$ for $\gamma < 1$ — we find that the RL
discount factor $\gamma$ can be interpreted directly as the economic discount rate:
a smaller $\gamma$ biases the policy toward early, high-revenue buyers.
Full NPV and time-cost sensitivity results are reported in the supplemental appendix.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Revenue}
\label{sec:exp_budget}

\paragraph{DP family comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:dp_family} compares the DP family on FF $n{=}1000$ across
$k \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40\}$.
Three qualitative findings stand out.
\emph{(i)} Cal-DP v3 dominates at $k \le 5$ (capital-binding regime):
at $k{=}3$ it achieves $99.8$ versus $45.2$ for Cal-DP v1 ($+120\%$)
and $8.4$ for Cal-DP v2. %SOURCE: dp_v3_full_curve_merged.json
\emph{(ii)} Cal-DP v2 dominates at $k \ge 8$ (budget-slack regime):
at $k{=}10$ it achieves $435.1$ versus $357.0$ for v3. %SOURCE: dp_v3_full_curve_merged.json
\emph{(iii)} The composite $\max$(v2, v3) dominates Cal-DP v1 at every $k$.

\paragraph{Budget-constrained learned policy vs.\ classical methods.}
Table~\ref{tab:budget_eval} compares all methods on FF $n{=}1000$
with production cost $c{=}0.3$.
The most striking result is at $k{=}1$ (budget $B_0{=}0.30$):
Greedy+Budget achieves only $7.4$ %SOURCE: dp_upgrade_eval.json
while the learned policy (Rev-GNN-LSTM with budget constraint) achieves $305.6$ %SOURCE: unified_sweep.json
— a $41{\times}$ factor.
This gap arises because Greedy+Budget goes bankrupt after its first rejection,
whereas the learned policy sets discounts just deep enough to ensure acceptance
and budget replenishment.

At $k{=}40$ the budget constraint is slack: Greedy+Budget achieves $448.7$, %SOURCE: dp_upgrade_eval.json
virtually matching the unconstrained optimum, and the advantage of the learned
policy narrows.

\paragraph{Regime map.}
Figure~\ref{fig:regime_map} plots revenue vs.\ $k$ for all methods.
Three distinct regimes emerge: (1) learned policy leads at $k \le 5$;
(2) composite Cal-DP leads at $6 \le k \le 15$;
(3) all non-oracle methods converge at $k \ge 20$.
On the denser Rice-Facebook graph, Cal-DP leads the learned policy across the
full mid-$k$ band — an honest finding we report transparently.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Effect of the LSTM module.}
Replacing the LSTM with a stateless GNN (zeroed context vector) reduces revenue
on FF $n{=}1000$ from $448.6$ to $435.9$ ($-2.8\%$), %SOURCE: paper_gen_updated.json (GNN-IM-RL vs LSTM)
confirming that episode history is valuable.
The gap widens on the Modular FF graph ($414.4$ vs.\ $328.5$, $-20.7\%$),
where module membership creates switching dynamics that the LSTM can track.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\columnwidth]{fig7_lstm_ablation}
  \caption{Ablation of the LSTM episode-history module.
    \textbf{Left:} Revenue on FF $n{=}1000$.
    \textbf{Right:} Revenue on Modular FF.
    Adding the LSTM brings a modest gain on FF ($+2.8\%$) but a large gain on
    Modular FF ($+26.2\%$), where community-switching patterns require
    history to price correctly.}
  \label{fig:lstm_ablation}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Effect of joint pricing.}
Removing the pricing head and substituting a fixed discount ($d{=}0.5$) for all
buyers reduces revenue by $12$–$18\%$ across all benchmarks, confirming that
joint seed-and-price optimization is essential for the gains reported above.
Details and full ablation table are in the supplemental appendix.
